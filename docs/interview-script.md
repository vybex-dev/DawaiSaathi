# Interview script — Dawai Saathi

Print this sheet. One interview takes about 10 minutes. Write only what the person actually says. Never write down medicine names or anything that identifies them.

## Before you start: consent (read aloud)

**English.** "Hello, I am building a small app to help people keep track of their medicines. May I ask you five short questions? There are no right or wrong answers. I will write down what you say, but I will not write your name, and I will not take your photo. You can skip any question or stop at any time, and nothing will change if you say no. Is it all right to begin?"

**हिन्दी (NEEDS NATIVE REVIEW).** "नमस्ते, मैं एक छोटा ऐप बना रहा हूँ जो लोगों को अपनी दवाइयों का ध्यान रखने में मदद करेगा। क्या मैं आपसे पाँच छोटे सवाल पूछ सकता हूँ? कोई जवाब सही या गलत नहीं है। आप जो कहेंगे मैं लिख लूँगा, पर आपका नाम नहीं लिखूँगा और आपकी फ़ोटो नहीं लूँगा। आप किसी भी सवाल को छोड़ सकते हैं या कभी भी रुक सकते हैं, और मना करने से कुछ नहीं बदलेगा। क्या मैं शुरू करूँ?"

Only continue if the person says yes. Write "consent: yes" at the top of your notes.

## The five questions

1. Show me how you take your medicines on a normal day.
2. Have you ever been unsure whether two medicines can be taken together? What did you do?
3. Who do you ask, and how long does it take to get an answer?
4. How do you read the small print on a strip? Glasses, light, someone else?
5. Would you rather hear the answer or read it, and in which language?

Hindi versions of the questions (NEEDS NATIVE REVIEW):

1. (NEEDS NATIVE REVIEW) "आप रोज़ अपनी दवाइयाँ कैसे लेते हैं? मुझे दिखाइए।"
2. (NEEDS NATIVE REVIEW) "क्या कभी आपको शक हुआ कि दो दवाइयाँ साथ में ली जा सकती हैं या नहीं? तब आपने क्या किया?"
3. (NEEDS NATIVE REVIEW) "आप किससे पूछते हैं, और जवाब मिलने में कितना समय लगता है?"
4. (NEEDS NATIVE REVIEW) "पत्ते पर छोटे अक्षर आप कैसे पढ़ते हैं? चश्मा, रोशनी, या कोई और पढ़कर बताता है?"
5. (NEEDS NATIVE REVIEW) "आप जवाब सुनना पसंद करेंगे या पढ़ना, और किस भाषा में?"

## Three tips for interviewing elderly people kindly

1. **Go at their pace.** Sit down next to them, speak clearly (not loudly), and leave silence after each question. Do not finish their sentences.
2. **Let them show, not just tell.** Ask them to show their strips or their routine. Do not touch, move or judge their medicines, and never tell them what to take or not take.
3. **End warmly.** Thank them, say they were helpful, and offer to answer any question about the project. If they seem tired or upset, stop early.
