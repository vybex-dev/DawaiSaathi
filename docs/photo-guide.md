# Photo guide — labelled strip set

## The rule

**Only photograph strips you own, or strips whose owner has said yes.** Ask first. If you are unsure, do not take the photo.

## One strip, one photo

1. Put the strip on a plain, flat, light surface (a sheet of white paper works).
2. Place it **printed side up**, flat, with nothing covering the print.
3. Use window light: sit near a window in daytime, with the light coming from the side so your hand and phone do not cast a shadow on the strip.
4. Hold the phone parallel to the strip, about a hand-span above it, so the strip fills most of the frame.
5. Tap the screen to focus on the printed text, then take the photo. Check by zooming in that you can read the brand name.
6. One strip per photo for this test set.

## Special groups

- **Clean (15):** the steps above, nothing unusual.
- **Torn (10):** use an **empty** strip (no tablets inside). Cut or tear off part of the print, for example the top or the corner with the brand name. Never cut a strip that still holds medicine.
- **Glare (8):** photograph an empty or full strip under a lamp so the foil shines. Take it from the angle where the glare covers part of the print.
- **Low light (4):** photograph in a dim room with no lamp pointed at the strip.
- **Hindi/bilingual (3):** strips that have Hindi (or another Indian language) text next to the English.
- **Look-alike (6):** pairs of strips with similar brand names but different ingredients. Photograph each strip of the pair separately and note the partner file in `notes`.

## What to crop out

Before you save a photo, crop out or cover anything personal:

- patient or doctor **names**
- **addresses**
- **phone numbers**
- barcodes or order numbers on pharmacy stickers

If a strip has a pharmacy sticker with personal details, photograph a different strip.

## File names

`g-001.jpg`, `g-002.jpg`, `g-003.jpg` and so on, in order. Save them in the ignored folder `private-strips/`. Add one row for each photo to `data/strips/labels.csv` right away, while the strip is still in front of you.
